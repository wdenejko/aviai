# Revision 2.1: the training and its gate

ADR-004, item 12. Revision 2.1 is Revision 2's mixture plus the recall round's 180 rows, retrained
from the base with Revision 2's recipe. It trained on dashi 2026-10-07/08, and its final adapter
went through the thinking-on mini-battery right after. The numbers are in
`20261008-rev2-1-gate.json`, beside Revision 2's for comparison.

## Summary

- **The gate passes, with no flag and no A/A flag.** Every change is within the base's own noise:

| Benchmark | Base | Revision 2.1 | Worse / better | p | A/A | Revision 2 |
|---|---:|---:|---|---:|---:|---:|
| IFEval | 88.0 | 87.0 | 10 / 8 | 0.82 | 87.5 | 86.0 |
| BFCL irrelevance | 81.7 | 79.2 | 9 / 6 | 0.61 | 78.3 | 82.5 |
| BIRD | 70.0 | 68.7 | 7 / 5 | 0.77 | 68.7 | 67.3 |
| HumanEval+ | 84.0 | 87.7 | 7 / 13 | 0.26 | 86.5 | 82.8 |

- **The training repeated Revision 2's:** 1,251 steps in 8 hours 37 minutes (24.8 seconds a
  step), train_loss 0.1553 (Revision 2: 0.1548). Per 100 steps, the mean loss stayed between
  0.148 and 0.172; Revision 2's stayed between 0.143 and 0.175.
- **Revision 2's shorter HumanEval+ reasoning is gone.** Revision 2's adapter reasoned 13.5% less
  than the base there (ratio 0.865, sign test p = 0.006), though the brevity flag needs a ratio
  under 0.8. Revision 2.1's ratio is 0.945, p = 0.74. Every ratio is now between 0.945 and
  1.021.
- **Unclosed reasoning moves within noise, both ways:** IFEval 2.5 → 4.0, BFCL 5.8 → 7.5, BIRD 0
  → 0, HumanEval+ 11.0 → 8.0. None is significant, and no reply opens a second reasoning block.
- **As for Revision 2, the gate shows no gain and none was expected.** None of the four benchmarks
  asks for what the mixture targets. The targeted tests, which follow, measure that.

## What was run

- **The training** (`rev2_train.sh`, `~/benchlab/runs/2026-10-07-qwen36-rev2-1-train/`; box clock
  2026-10-07 22:32 to 2026-10-08 07:09):
  - **Recipe and data:** rank 4, learning rate 1e-4, one epoch over Revision 2.1's 1,251 blocks
    (PR #51).
  - **Order:** armed behind the recall round's test window, so it started when that window had
    ended and OCR was back.
  - **Output:** a checkpoint every 100 steps, all kept, and final/.
- **The export:** `~/benchlab/scripts/rev2-train/gate_chain.sh` waited on the box for the trainer
  to exit rc = 0 and for the training window to restore OCR. Then it ran
  `mini-battery/checkpoint.sh` on final/: 660 adapter tensors became 740 GGUF tensors (f32), as for
  Revision 2. Last, it launched the gate's window.
- **The gate** (`battery_window.sh`, `~/benchlab/runs/2026-10-08-qwen36-rev2-1-gate-final/`; box
  clock 07:10 to 12:34, 5 hours 24 minutes):
  - **Passes and timing:** the adapter's passes only, with the base's, A/A and gold passes linked
    from the calibration (2026-10-02). BFCL took 38 minutes, IFEval 99, HumanEval+ 132, BIRD 53.
  - **Scoring:** IFEval and BFCL on the host, HumanEval+ and BIRD in the podman sandbox; then
    `mini summary` against the base, with base_rep as the A/A.
- **The raw scores:** BFCL 104/120, IFEval 174/200, HumanEval+ 143/164, BIRD 103/150.
  - The summary's BFCL is lower (79.2%) because it counts a reply whose reasoning never closed as
    a failure, as the calibration decided.
- **One box event:** OCR was restored at the end of the training and was still loading when the
  gate stopped it. It outlived the stop and the window killed it, the case `stop_ocr` exists for.
  It was restored at the window's end.

## Reading it

- **Revision 2.1 keeps what the base does, as Revision 2 did.** The round's 199 added Target A rows
  (3.6% of the tokens) moved nothing the gate measures.
- **The difference from Revision 2 is noise either way.** BFCL 82.5 → 79.2 and HumanEval+ 82.8 →
  87.7 both sit inside the A/A's spread, and both adapters were tested against the same base
  passes. The brevity signal is the one measured change, and it went away.
- **Next are the targeted tests, queued behind the gate:**
  - the round's test and the Target A test, each base against Revision 2.1, paired;
  - then the probe and dsbench through pi.

  DuckDB's cells and dsbench's `da_weekend_delay` are where the round should show.
